# Kopano Match: the investor view

Figures marked [verify] are placeholders. Do not present them as facts until they are sourced.

## The problem
Small businesses lose contracts and funding for reasons nobody tells them, usually paperwork. Corporates
and funders spend heavily on enterprise and supplier development and cannot find credible, ready
suppliers. Both sides lose time, and the money goes to whoever already has the paperwork.

## What we built
A matching platform that shows every match as **two scores**: fit and readiness. When a business is not
ready, it says exactly what is missing, who issues it, how long it takes and what it costs. Corporates get a
ranked shortlist with reasons, consortium assembly when no single business can deliver, structured
feedback, and an impact report. YES gets a programme view of demand, supply gaps and outcomes.

## Why it is different
Most platforms match or verify. We **unlock**.

1. **Costed, dated gaps.** "Tax PIN, 7 days, R0, opens R4.2m." Nobody else turns a rejection into a plan.
2. **The Readiness Passport.** A shareable proof page the business owns. It travels outside the platform,
   so every business brings its buyers in. That is the growth loop.
3. **Explainable by design.** Scores are rules, not a model. Fairness is tested and published, including the
   one group that falls outside the four-fifths rule. AI reads and drafts, a person decides.
4. **Demonstrated impact.** "Awarded despite a document gap" is a number a strict filter can never produce.

## The moat
The data that compounds: which credentials unlock which contracts, how long they really take, and a
buyer-verified track record built from disputable reviews. A competitor can copy the screens. It cannot
copy a delivery history.

## Business model (hypotheses to test)
| Who pays | For | Why they pay |
|---|---|---|
| Corporates and funders | A seat plus a fee per opportunity or award | Qualified shortlists, lower onboarding cost, impact reporting for ESD and B-BBEE scorecards |
| Credential providers (accountants, consultants, issuers) | Referral fee when a business buys a credential through an unlock plan | The platform sends them ready-to-buy customers with a dated need |
| Development programmes such as YES | Programme licence | Ecosystem dashboard, targeted support where the gaps are |
| Businesses | Free to start; premium unlock services later | Keeps supply high |

## Go to market
Start with YES as the anchor programme and its youth-owned businesses. Add three to five corporates
running enterprise and supplier development. Use the passport so each business invites its own buyers.

## Traction we can show now, and what we need
- **Have:** working product, tested engine, public fairness audit, demo data.
- **Need before investors take it seriously:** real businesses (even 20), one real buyer pilot, verified
  credential costs and turnaround times, a signed operator agreement for email and AI, an appointed
  Information Officer.

## Risks, stated plainly
- Data is synthetic today. Real-world accuracy is unproven until a pilot produces outcomes.
- Verification uses mock sources in the demo. Live registry, tax and identity integrations are the next build.
- Buyers may resist sharing procurement data. Integration is phased, starting with the CSV and API feed.
- POPIA: built against its conditions, not claimed compliant.
